## The three patterns

Worth more than any single finding, because they predict where the next one will be.

1. **The copy is honest wherever it is EXPLAINING, and overclaims wherever it is CONSOLING.** The wizard,
   `help-data.jsx`, `TROUBLESHOOTING.md` and `SAFEGUARDING.md` are unusually good — *"If these words are lost,
   the church is gone — not locked, gone."* Every divergence found is at a moment of reassurance: the
   forced-PIN gate, the Today nudge, the lock screen, the care-request sheet.
2. **Honest handling was built once, on one screen, and never propagated.** `joinState` distinguishes "we
   could not check" from "the answer is no" — and is consumed by one file. `clearanceKnown` is computed and has
   **zero** consumers. The `sealed` treatment ("Details hidden — this device isn't on the care team's key
   list") was written for undecryptable content and wired to Care only.
3. **Weight is inverted.** Marking a child costs the same two taps as posting a notice. Blocking is a two-step
   confirm; removing a 12-year-old's protection is one unguarded tap. Deleting a chat room has a 9-second undo.
   A chat message gets 50 persisted retries; "I need help" gets one shot.

## Ranked

### 1. The Bible bundled in the APK is never read `[V]`

`engine.js:400` — on native `ASSET_BASE = "https://app.trinityone.church/"` and `resolveAsset` rewrites every
relative module URL to it. `fetchAndCacheModule` (`engine.js:427`) does a SINGLE `fetch(resolveAsset(url))`
with no fallback to the relative path. Capacitor serves `www/modules/engbsb.zip` fine at
`https://localhost/modules/engbsb.zip` — nothing ever asks for it.

Verified the file really does ship: `3,021,299` bytes at `assets/public/modules/engbsb.zip` inside
`trinityone.apk`. The comment justifying the rewrite (`engine.js:394-396`) still says *"the APK ships none"* —
written before the file was added, never revisited.

**Experience:** the APK arrives by Quick Share in a place with no connection — the documented route for exactly
that place — and Read says *"You appear to be offline. Connect to the internet and your Bible will download
automatically."* The offline-first promise, which is the product's centre, does not hold.

**And the guard test says it does.** `scripts/offline-shell.test.mjs:53` asserts the `cp` line exists in
`sync-web.sh`. It never asserts the file is READ. False assurance since 2026-07-27.

Aggravating `[R]`: that fetch has no timeout, and the `online` self-heal is gated on `!loadingFlag`, so a hung
first download disables recovery permanently while a cleanly failed one recovers.

### 2. A child cannot message their own parent `[V]`

`docs/security/SAFEGUARDING.md` promises *"the child can message their parent"*. `app/app.jsx:1626` derives
that exemption from `safeguard.guardians` — which `scripts/gateway.mjs` serves only to
`authed === churchpub || stewardOf || careAdmin || networkOf`. The engine says so itself: *"that map is
deliberately served only to stewards now"*. On a child's device the map is permanently `{}`, `linked` is
permanently false, and the composer is replaced with:

> "For safeguarding, private messaging is limited here. Speak with your church leaders if you need to get in touch."

The relay WOULD have allowed it (`guardianLinkedIn`). The client refuses first. Asymmetric: the parent is not a
minor, so the parent can write and the child cannot reply.

**Why it matters most:** in the exact scenario where a child has a concern about someone at church, the app
routes them away from their family and toward church leaders. A privacy fix removed the map from members and
never updated its consumer.

**Fix shape:** put the parent list in the child's OWN sealed `clearance:` doc, which they already read and
already trust for `minor`/`cleared`. Survives offline too. Needs a test that fails against today's code.

### 3. A forgotten PIN tells the member to destroy their account `[V]` — found independently by two reviewers

`app/identity.jsx:820`: *"reinstall the app and restore your account with your 12 words."* Unconditional. With
`allowBackup=false` a reinstall is a clean wipe.

The engine disagrees, in a comment written for this case — `src/identity.src.js:316`: *"RECOVERY ALWAYS WINS:
importing clears any community-PIN lock… so a forgotten PIN can NEVER trap the key."* Typing 12 words at the
lock screen would work TODAY. `PinUnlockGate` has no mnemonic input (verified: zero matches), and the route to
one is disabled while locked (`onProfile={commLocked ? undefined : …}`).

**The console fixed exactly this on 2026-07-30 and ships the pattern** (`StewardUnlock`'s "Restore this church
with my 12 words"). The member app never got it. The app also KNOWS whether they backed up
(`trinityone.backedup.<npub>`) and never consults it.

### 4. The child's phone fails open to "adult" `[R, mechanism V]`

`app/app.jsx:1095` initial state is `isMinor: false`; the safeguarding subscription sits behind a 1200 ms
`lazyReady` deferral; the group list paints immediately from localStorage cache, ungated; `safeguard` is never
persisted. A PIN-locked boot reads anonymously, so the `clearance:` doc (which requires NIP-42) never arrives —
"adult" persists until unlock. Offline, indefinitely.

`src/fellowship.src.js:2554` emits **`clearanceKnown`** — the exact tri-state that separates "confirmed adult"
from "we don't know yet". **Zero consumers in `app/`** `[V]`.

### 5. Joining a church is fire-and-forget `[V]`

`announceMembership` ends `try { await _publishAny(...) } catch { console.warn(...) }`. No outbox — `_outbox.push`
exists at exactly two sites, group chat and DMs. The heartbeat retry is throttled to 12 h and marks itself done
on ATTEMPT. "Check again" re-runs the read subscription, not the announce.

Meanwhile: *"Your request to join X has been sent… there's nothing to do in the meantime."* The one honest
instruction is the one that guarantees they stay invisible. Fires on first launch, on a thin pipe.

`[R]` Declined and ignored render identically, forever — the member app never subscribes to the blocklist, so a
declined member sits on *"a steward usually lets people in within a day"* indefinitely. Three states —
never-arrived, waiting, refused — render as one.

### 6. The steward backup ceremony cannot be completed `[V]`

`app/backup.jsx:39` — `PASS_MIN = 12`. `app/stew-dashboard.jsx:3833` gates on `PASS_MIN` but the error says
**"Use at least 4 characters (a numeric PIN is fine)."** The button enables at `pass.length < 4`. So a steward
typing 4–11 characters is told to type at least 4, and refused, in a loop. The strength meter reports "Strong"
for an 8-digit numeric passphrase `checkPass` will reject.

This is the only flow that saves the church key to a file — *"The one irreplaceable thing."*

Same panel `[V]`: *"If you forget this, the backup can't be opened — not even by us. **Store it with the
file.**"* Under a seizure threat model that instructs the steward to nullify the encryption.

### 7. A delegated steward's Block is silently discarded — and the console says it worked `[V]`

`setBlocked` signs with `sk`, which for a delegate is their own device key; the read-back filter is
`_byChurch = (e) => e.pubkey === pub` — owner-only. Their own console drops their write; the member stays and
keeps posting. The UI says *"They have been removed from the roster…"*. They were not. The same path is the ✕
on a join request, so a delegate can approve but silently cannot decline.

`[V]` And a delegate who reloads loses the church: `setActiveIdentity` is called from one place (the switcher)
and persisted nowhere, so every reload boots them into their own empty identity — "Your Church", zero members.
The instinct that triggers ("Restore a church") is the wrong action.

### 8. Safeguarding's authoritative lists get the loose publish path `[R]`

`setMinors` / `setApproved` / `setGuardians` use `publish()` = `Promise.any` — resolves when ONE relay accepts.
`_publishToRelays` (all-must-accept + verify read-back + dedicated banner) is wired to `publishClearance` only.
So the DERIVED client-side hint has all three protections; the documents the RELAY enforces on have none. No
healing: `steward-relay-returned` re-runs clearances only.

Severity scales with relay count — measure the pilot church's actual `relays()` before deciding urgency.

### 9. "Ask for help" states an audience that is not true `[R]`

*"This goes privately to your care team — no one else sees it."* Actual read set is author + church key + every
steward + every care-admin. And if the `careteam:` query returns empty (offline, slow, or none configured) the
request seals to church + self only, the toast still says "Sent to your care team", and the care team cannot
open it. There is a loud misconfiguration warning for needs and none for requests.

`[V]` Delivery is one shot: `publishCareRequest` has no outbox. Small talk gets 50 retries.

### 10. Care goes silent after submission `[R]`

Sorted newest-first, so the oldest un-triaged request sinks. No age rendered anywhere. Urgency captured and
never used. No unread, no SLA, no reminder, no expiry, no empty state (both queues `return null`, so "none" is
identical to "misconfigured"). The member is never notified of approve/decline. Nothing in the product will
resurface a 2am request that nobody opened.

### 11. Marking a child is one silent tap `[R]`

No confirmation either direction, no undo, no success feedback. The effect lives in a `title=` attribute, which
does not render on touch, and the standing explanation is a `DismissibleNote` whose dismissal is permanent per
device with no way back.

`[V]` And it can empty the child's app: `NewGroupModal` defaults `childsafe` to false and the wizard's starter
groups carry no flag, so the standard path leaves a marked teenager with **zero** visible groups, silently.

### 12. The child is told nothing, and the empty state blames the church `[R]`

Two safeguarding strings exist in the whole member app, both framing the limit as a property of the
conversation, never the account. When every group is filtered out the child gets *"{church} hasn't opened any
chat rooms yet"* — false, and it blames the church.

Hiding the badge from peers is right. "We will not tell you the rules that apply to you" is a different
decision, made silently.

### 13. Ceremonies without gates `[R]`

- **"Reveal recovery phrase"** — one tap, no PIN re-entry, no auto-hide, with a Copy button. "Remove lock"
  requires the current PIN; revealing the whole church requires nothing.
- **Child-account phrase** — shown once, no ack, no quiz, discarded by one "Done" tap, no re-reveal path. The
  account most likely to be lost gets the least ceremony.
- **`window.prompt`** for the passphrase to a church-key restore.
- **"Start over"** on the steward-code screen reloads, discarding a memory-only identity whose code may already
  have been handed to the owner.

## The finding that generalises phase 1's pattern

Phase 1 said honest handling was built once and never propagated. Phase 2 measured it:

* `EmptyState` — the ONE shared component with real loading / error / offline branches — is used **exactly once**
  in the whole member app (the Read tab). `looksOffline()` has one consumer: `EmptyState`. `Spinner` has three
  call sites; every other spinner is a hand-rolled inline div (≥8 copies). `[R]`
* `trinity-feature-failed` — the global "something didn't load" banner — is dispatched from **one** place in the
  transport, and only when a church-doc handler *throws*. **No `app/*.jsx` file ever dispatches it.** A network
  failure or a never-resolving subscription never reaches it. `[R]`
* **7 of 10 content surfaces have no error state at all.** Only Listen, Watch and Read do. Only Watch offers a
  Retry. Only Chat consumes `ctx.retryConnection`. `[R]`
* **No surface has a not-yet-admitted state except Chat**, though `joinState` sits on `ctx`. A pending member
  sees empty Serving, Events, Plans and Watch — never "Waiting for approval". `[R]`

The root cause is mechanical: the church-data subscriptions hand the UI a bare array with **no ready/error/eosed
flag**, so "resolved empty", "rejected" and "never answered" produce identical React state. Only
`subscribeChurchMembers` (`done`) and `subscribeChurchJoin` (`loaded`) do better.

## Worst individual states

1. **Module download is the highest-risk spinner in the app.** `engine.js` has no `AbortController`, no timeout
   and no progress: `res.arrayBuffer()` buffers 3–9 MB with zero progress events, so the row shows the same
   34px spinner at 0% and 99%. There is no cancel. And the retry is worse than useless — `installing.has(url)`
   early-returns while the dead fetch is still pending, so the retry tap resolves `undefined` and fires the
   **success** toast: *"Installed X"* for a download that never completed. `[R]`
2. **The mirror catalogue fails to a permanent spinner** — `getMirror()` catches to `null`, and the UI's null
   test IS the loading test. The promise is memoised, so it never retries for the app's lifetime. `[R]`
3. **The module store fails to a blank page** — `getCatalog()` catches every failure into `{categories: []}`,
   giving an empty scroll area with no spinner, no text and no error. `[R]`
4. **Watch converts a dead gateway into a successful empty result** (`.catch(() => done({videos:[]}))`), which
   disarms its own watchdog and renders "Nothing to watch yet" for a network failure. `[R]`
5. `ChatRoom`, `DMThread` and `DMInbox` have **no spinner and no timeout** — hung, slow and empty are one
   identical blank scroller. `DMThread`'s send is fire-and-forget with no outbox bubble, unlike `ChatRoom`. `[R]`
6. **Care has no timeout or spinner anywhere**, and `CareCard` evaluates `if (!s.enabled) return null` against a
   hard-coded default of `false`, so a Care-enabled church renders nothing at all until the relay answers. `[R]`

The counter-example the codebase already got right is **Listen** (`screens-extras.jsx:196`), which branches on
`data.error` and says *"This is your connection, not your church."* That pattern exists on exactly one surface.

* **The PIN feature carries six names** — Identity lock, Community PIN, protection, PIN-lock, "church community

* *"What doesn't: your old private messages and anything sealed to you… That is why they were private."* —

## The copy thesis, refined

Phase 1 said "honest when explaining, overclaims when consoling." The detail pass **refined that, and it is a
better rule**: the product consoles honestly at length (`stew-meals.jsx:673` volunteers *"no one will see this
need"* in bold), and overclaims inside explainers (`help-data.jsx:17`). The real predictor is:

> **Distance from the code that implements the claim.** Copy written NEXT TO the mechanism is honest. Copy
> written ABOUT a mechanism one or more files away overclaims.

And a sharper second pattern: **every confirmed overclaim is an AUDIENCE or DELIVERY claim, never a
key/recovery one.** Recovery copy is uniformly excellent across six files. *Who can see this* and *this has been
sent* are where it fails — because both truths live in `gateway.mjs`'s `canRead()` or in a swallowed `catch {}`,
neither visible from the JSX.

### Three copy fixes standing in for CODE fixes

1. **The safety roll-call is readable by one person, and says "leaders"** `[V]`. `markSafe` does
   `_dmEncrypt(sk, check.by, body)` — encrypted to the **event signer, a single key**. When a delegated steward
   starts the check, only that one volunteer's browser can ever decrypt *"I need help."* Other stewards and the
   owner see nothing. The member is told: *"Only your church's leaders can see your reply — not other members,
   and not the server."* A life-safety feature failing in the direction that costs someone help, with the plural
   concealing it. **Code fix: encrypt to the church key, not the signer.**
2. **Serving replies are cleartext, under a shield icon** `[R]`. `respondToServingRequest` publishes
   `{request, v, swapTo}` unencrypted in a kind-30078; `canRead`'s tail returns true for any authenticated
   member. The UI says *"Only your team leader sees your reply."* Every member can read it, and so can a seized
   relay. **Code fix: encrypt `reqreply:`.**
3. **Five serving toasts fire before anything is sent** `[R]` — the publish is `catch {}`-swallowed and the
   toast is unawaited. *"Declined — your leader has been told"* is a claim about another person's knowledge with
   the publish result discarded. `markSafe` already solved this with a real ACK and a comment explaining why;
   serving never got it.

Also confirmed: **"Everything stays on this device — nothing is uploaded"** on the Read tab — the first screen a
new user sees — while `mydata.src.js` publishes highlights, notes, journal and prayer list to relays (encrypted,
which is good, but "encrypted" is not "nothing"). And a **"Private / Public" chip on every note whose flag no
code reads** — all six doc types publish `priv: true` unconditionally. Inert in both directions, including the
one where a user believes they have shared something.

**"Restore" now denotes six operations**, not two — including "close this need and open it again" in a care
sheet, next to a Restore that replaces the church key.

same roster. So **8 of 9 console panels render `[]` identically whether the church is empty or the relay is
down** — and `PublishErrorBanner`, the console's only failure surface, listens to two *write* events. A failed
read raises nothing. The console is well-built for "your change didn't save" and silent for "your church didn't
load."

### Five changes that fix the most for the least

1. `role="status" aria-live="polite"` on `Toast` + a `tone` prop that swaps the tick — one component, ~110 call
   sites, and it closes the tick-over-failure lie at 28+ of them.
2. Generalise `EmptyState` into a shared `<DataState loading error empty>`, and wire `stewardStreamLoaded` into
   the 35 console subscription hooks so the signal is free rather than opt-in at 3.
3. Convert the 25 raw icon buttons to `IconBtn` and add the 6 missing names to `ICON_LABELS`.
4. Apply `useDialogA11y` at the 10 hand-rolled modal sites — 3 already have the ARIA and need only the ref.
5. A `<Field label=…>` wrapper emitting `<label htmlFor>`; four files account for 146 of the 231.

## Visual system — the design system was never finished, not abandoned

`brand.css` defines 18 colour tokens, 4 radii, 3 shadows, 3 font families. It defines **no type scale and no
spacing scale at all.** So across 26,038 lines of `app/*.jsx`:

| Axis | Tokenised | Hardcoded | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colour | 7,084 `var(--…)` | 773 | **90%** |
| Font size | **0** | 2,472 | **0%** |
| Border radius | 2 | 1,402 | **0.1%** |
| Padding / spacing | 0 | 1,748 | **0%** |

The one axis with tokens is 90% compliant. The three without are 0%. **37 distinct font sizes; 40% of all
font-size declarations are half-pixel values** (`12.5`, `13.5`, `14.5`…) — the signature of per-site nudging
with no scale to snap to. The 4 radius tokens that exist cover ~8% of real use, and the most-used value (12px)
has no token: the scale was authored without measuring the code.

**Three tokens are missing and it shows** `[V]`:
* `--shadow` is used **12 times in the console** and defined **0 times** in `brand.css` (it exists only in
  `index.html`). Those `box-shadow`s are dropped entirely — including an `onMouseEnter` hover affordance that
  therefore does nothing.
* `--gold-ink` doesn't exist, so **eight files each re-derived the same hand-darkened `#8a6717`** (37 uses)
  because `--gold` is 2.63:1 and fails AA.
* `--sage-ink` is undefined, so a success message renders in body ink while its error sibling renders red.

**The design system has one consumer.** Only 4 of 16 HTML files link `brand.css`; the rest inline a copy. Three
drifted marketing palettes now exist and the brand's primary orange renders as three different colours across
the public site. The drifted one is the *accessible* one: `#fff` on canonical `--clay` is 4.36:1 (fail), on the
marketing `#b4533f` it is 4.94:1 (pass).

**The previous a11y fix caused a regression** `[R]`: `--ink-3` was darkened and measured only against light
surfaces, but it is also the footer colour on `--midnight`. Pages that link `brand.css` now fail in the footer
(3.45:1); pages that inlined the old value still fail in the body (2.29–2.60:1). Every marketing page fails
somewhere, in opposite directions.

### Each app has exactly the half the other lacks `[V]`

* The **member app** has a hardware-back stack across 27 overlay layers — and **no focus trap or Escape** on any
  real modal. `Modal` and `Sheet`, the two primitives that carry `useDialogA11y`, have **1 call site repo-wide**.
  They are dead code, which is why the hook "being unused" looked true.
* The **console** has focus trap + Escape via `useStewDialog` at 30 sites — and **no hardware-back** on 47 of 48

So Back closes sheets in the app but **exits the console**; Escape closes console dialogs but does nothing in
the app.

**Body-scroll lock: 0 sites in 26,038 lines** `[V]`. Not one modal in either app locks background scroll — on
Android WebView the single biggest contributor to "this feels broken".

**Pressed state: 0 of 862 buttons** `[V]`. `:active` is defined **zero times** in `index.html`, `steward.html`
and `brand.css`. Both button systems spend their whole feedback budget on `:hover { transform }`, which never

**`--clay` is both the primary CTA and the destructive colour** — `brand.css` documents four roles and none of

## Addendum, found while fixing #2 — two notes

**1. A tempting fix for the child→parent DM is UNSAFE, and must not be taken.** The obvious small change is to
have the child's app derive its parents from the `guardreq:<childpub>` documents — they are p-tagged to the
child and `canRead` has no branch for them, so they fall to the default member rule and the child can read them
today. **Do not.** A guardian request is authored by the *claimed* parent. `accept()` enforces only that the
author is who they say they are (SECURITY-AUDIT-2026-07-20 C1) — not that they are that child's parent. The
confirmation exists solely in the church's `guardians:` map. A client that trusted `guardreq` would let **any
adult self-declare as a child's parent and bypass the DM restriction entirely** — a grooming primitive.

The only safe source is the church: put the confirmed parent pubkeys in the child's own `clearance:` doc, which
is NIP-44-sealed by the church key to that child and already carries `minor`/`cleared`. It tells the child only
about themselves, survives offline, and cannot be forged by a peer.

**2. `guardreq:` documents are readable by every member of the church** `[V]`. `canRead` has no `GUARDREQ_D`
branch, so they fall to the default member rule (any admitted, non-blocked member). The engine comment says the
guardians MAP is "deliberately served only to stewards now (it maps every child in the congregation to their
parents)" — but the individual requests it is built from are readable by the whole congregation, so the
parent↔child graph can be enumerated by any member. That substantially undercuts the stated protection. Gate
`guardreq:` reads to the church, its stewards, the author, and the p-tagged child.
