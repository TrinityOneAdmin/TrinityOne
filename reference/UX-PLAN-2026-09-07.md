**Every item below was re-checked by me on the running app.** Where the round's report was wrong, the
correction is stated. Two of its five headline findings did not survive verification unchanged, and one
would have undone a deliberate decision of the owner's.

## 1. The console tells a steward their invite will not work — and it does work
**VERIFIED. Highest cost of anything here: it stops people joining at all.**

Console (Invite your church):
> "Right now your relay only answers on this building's wifi, so an invite would fail on anyone else's
> phone. Turn on a secure tunnel … and your church becomes reachable worldwide."

Relay control panel, same machine, at the same moment:
> "Reach members from anywhere … **ON · PUBLIC** … Reachable from anywhere
> https://trinityone.tailbeaac0.ts.net"

The tunnel is on. I have been using it from the phone all day.

**Cause:** the console judges reachability from its OWN address. `ownIsLoopback()`
(`steward.src.js:~600`) tests whether `ownRelay()` looks like `localhost/127.0.0.1`, and this console is
pointed at `127.0.0.1:8000`, so it answers "wifi only". It never asks the relay, which knows.

**The change.** The console must ask the relay rather than infer from its own URL. The relay already
publishes the answer — its panel renders "ON · PUBLIC" and the public URL from the same box. `/status`
is already fetched by the console for other purposes; the tunnel state should ride with it (or a small
`/public-url` on the same admin-gated path the control panel uses). Then:
- tunnel on -> show the public invite and say nothing about wifi;
- tunnel off -> keep today's wording, which is correct in that case;
- cannot tell -> say "we could not check" and offer the invite anyway. **Never assert "would fail".**

**Callers to enumerate before touching:** `joinUrl()` (`steward.src.js:7400`) and everything rendering
the invite panel; `ownIsLoopback`, `selfPublicRelay`, `setSelfPublicRelay` and the `steward-relays` event
they dispatch. Note `joinUrl()` already prefers a public base and falls back — the data is nearly there.

**Test at the point of use:** render the invite panel with the relay reporting public, and assert the
"would fail on anyone else's phone" sentence is NOT on screen. Sabotage: force the relay answer to
"unknown" and assert it does not claim failure.

## 2. The relay tells a volunteer to restart a healthy server
**VERIFIED, and it is the first line on every tab of the panel.**

    Relay not reachable | Is the relay running? Restart the app.

Directly below, same page: `ON · PUBLIC`, `✓ Reachable from anywhere`, `294 events · busiest day 207`,
`Church records 255 · 87%`, two churches listed. `/status` returns `ok: true`.

**Cost:** this audience is a volunteer who lent a laptop. The one instruction on screen is to restart the
thing that is working, and there is no way for them to tell that the banner is wrong.

**The change.** The banner must not outrank the evidence beneath it. Three parts:
- Only show "not reachable" when the panel's own health probe has FAILED — not when it has not answered
  yet. An unanswered probe is "checking…", not "down".
- If any live figure on the page is non-zero, the banner is contradicted by definition: suppress it and
  show what was actually observed ("last contact 3s ago").
- Drop "Restart the app" unless we know restarting helps. Replace with what to check, in order.

**Do not simply hide it.** A genuinely unreachable relay must still say so — this audience has no other
signal.

## 3. Four of six dashboard numbers are dashes, beside the figures they claim not to know
**VERIFIED.** Cards read value-above-label:

    —  Churches        —  Members       —  Events stored
    —  Connected now   87  Sent today   0  Media stored

While the same page shows `294 events`, `Church records 255 · 87%`, `Profiles 25`, and lists two churches
by name. **Correction to the round's report:** its first pass misread the card order and thought
"Connected now" was 57/85; the value sits above the label, so Connected now is a dash and 87 is Sent today.
The second pass caught this. I confirmed the second pass is right.

**The change.** Either compute these from the same source the sections below already use, or remove the
cards. A dash means "unknown"; printing it next to the answer is worse than printing nothing.

## 4. A member is never told they have been let in — and the help promises they will be
**VERIFIED.** The admitted member's screen contains no admission notice; the waiting card simply
disappeared. Help says:

> "Leave the app open and you will see it happen."

Someone who follows that instruction exactly sees nothing happen.

**The change (small, high value).** On the transition from waiting to admitted, show a confirmation that
survives a few seconds and is dismissible — the project's own rule is that confirmations are moments, so
collapse it after ~5s but leave the church reachable behind it. If that is not built, the help sentence
must change instead. **One or the other; today the app and the help disagree.**

## 5. The same church key has two petnames that differ — "Patient Brook 10" vs "Patient Brook 9460"
**VERIFIED, and the round's framing was WRONG in a way that matters.**

The report said the relay should show real church names. **It must not.** `reference/DOMAIN.md:125`
records the owner's decision of 2026-09-04: *"A relay must not hold a church's NAME. It gets a petname
derived from the key instead."* Doing what the report asked would undo a deliberate privacy protection.

What IS wrong is narrower and real. Both surfaces derive a petname from the same key with the same hash
and the same two wordlists — so the words agree — but the numeric suffix does not:

    gateway.mjs:469     churchPetName()   four digits  ->  "Patient Brook 9460"
    steward.src.js:1368 stewardNameFor()  two digits   ->  "Patient Brook 10"

Each is defensible alone; the relay's own comment explains why it needs four digits (collision rates at
100–400 churches). Nobody considered the two being read side by side. A warden comparing them sees
different numbers and concludes they are different things — which is exactly the confusion the label
exists to prevent.

**The change.** Do not unify the entropy — the relay needs it. Make the relationship legible instead:
show the word pair as the identity and the number as a quiet disambiguator, and have the console display
the church's petname in the SAME form the relay uses, next to the npub, where an operator would compare
them. Cheapest correct version: one shared derivation exported once, rendering "Patient Brook" prominently
with the suffix subdued.

## 6. Full-screen pages do not show the way back — six reports, five screens, two rounds
**VERIFIED as a pattern; every individual report was FALSE and I withdrew each.**

Help (×2, round 3), Search (round 3), Serving (×2, round 4), a chat room (round 4). In each case the
screen was working correctly and the user concluded they were trapped. Screenshot evidence:
`round4/9510-blocked.png` shows a correct Serving page with an honest empty state.

The common factor: a full-screen view replaces everything, the bottom navigation is gone, and the only
exit is an unlabelled `‹`. On a phone the OS back gesture rescues you — I verified that. In the console,
and for anyone who does not think to swipe, the chevron is the whole affordance.

**The change.** Give the chevron a visible word and name the destination — "‹ Today", "‹ Console".
Apply to Help, Search, Serving, and room views. The console Help dialog additionally has no visible
close at all (two agents independently used browser Back to escape it).

**Do not dismiss this because the reports were wrong.** Six users, five screens, independently. The
misreadings are the evidence.

## What is good and must not be changed

- **The relay Settings legend** — "Applies immediately — confirmed by the relay · Needs Save — review,
  then apply together · Can't be undone — asks you to confirm". Verified on screen. This is the best
  pattern in the product.
- **The red Restore warning** — "This replaces everything — every church's messages, files and lists are
  overwritten by the snapshot … It cannot be undone."
- **The wifi consequence line** — "Off is safer. On, anyone on the same network — including a guest or
  church-hall wifi — can reach this relay." Describes the consequence, prescribes nothing.
- **Capability refusals in the console** — "Safeguarding isn't yours to run … Nothing is broken and you
  haven't done anything wrong. Ask whoever holds the church key."
- **Correction to the round's report:** it claimed the console Security tab lacked the advice about not
  sharing the key. It does not — "To add a helper without giving away the key, use Delegated stewards
  below" is present. Its faults are ordering and a wrong "below", not missing advice.
- **Destructive styling is better than reported.** "Remove this church from this device" IS red
  (`rgb(168,70,42)`); the others are neutral. The report's "four identical grey pills" is wrong. The real
  issue is only that "Restore from a recovery phrase", which overwrites the church identity, looks
  exactly like "Back up to a file", which is harmless — and it does carry a confirm.

# VERIFICATION PASS — 2026-09-07, after the first three fixes

## #6 "full-screen pages are traps" — DOWNGRADED from a defect to a design question

Six agents across two rounds concluded they were trapped. Every individual report was false and each was
withdrawn. Checking what is actually there before changing 45 call sites:

- **Console Help**: has a plainly visible `×` in the top-right (`stew-help.jsx:67`, aria-label "Close
  help"), screenshotted at `round4/console-help-close.png`. The report that it "lacks a visible close
  button — users must use browser back navigation" is simply wrong.
- **Member Help**: a bare `‹` chevron with an aria-label and no visible word. On a phone the OS back
  gesture also works — verified on the Oppo.
- **The rest**: `Icon name="chevL"` appears at 45 sites across app/*.jsx. Most are ordinary sub-page
  backs where the destination is obvious.

So the concrete defect is one unlabelled chevron on one screen, not a systemic trap. Changing 45 call
sites would be a design decision with real layout risk (a word beside a chevron wraps at the largest text
size on a narrow phone), taken on evidence that did not survive checking.

**What remains true:** six people did get confused, and the confusion is worth taking seriously — but as
a design question for the owner, not as a bug to fix unasked. Recommended if the owner wants it: name the
destination on the screens people actually got lost on ("‹ Today"), not everywhere.

## #4 "a member is never told they are let in" — REAL, and fixed

The mechanism existed and was well built (fires on pending→admitted, guards reconnect flicker with a
persisted per-church flag). It required having WATCHED the transition — the app open, having seen the
pending state first. A steward takes up to a day and the help tells the member closing the app is fine,
so the common case reopened to a church that simply worked, with nothing ever saying they were in.

Now fires on the standing state. Safe because `isAdmitted` is a positive signal read live: a refused or
gated read returns EMPTY, so it can be falsely negative but never falsely positive.
