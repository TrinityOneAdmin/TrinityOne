The one rule that orders everything below

Never describe a protection as more than it is. An honest limit survives a police visit; an overclaim gets someone charged. Where a task exists to make a claim true, it outranks a task that makes the product nicer.

Before anything ships Now

Everything from the last three days is merged and passing 471 automated tests, and none of it has been run on a phone. That is the only thing between here and a pilot.

  • Work the device checklist end to endBlocks all
    The existing checklist covers the recovery work but predates the name encryption. It needs a second half.
    Why it can't be compressed: the last two days found twelve faults that looked perfect on paper, and the worst one — the roster blanking people's names — was found by reading code, not by any test.
    • Names appear: in chat, the member directory, the steward console
    • Someone awaiting approval shows a readable name, not gibberish
    • Set up a child account — the leader sees the child's name
    • Block a member — everyone else's names still work, history intact
    • Restore from 12 words — name and churches both come back
    • Phone-to-phone transfer, both handsets present
    • A child's app knows it is a child (the clearance fix)
    • Airplane mode: reload the app, it still opens
  • Test on the phone your audience actually hasUnusual
    A cheap Android five years old, throttled to 2G, with the screen in a language you don't read.
    Why: every device test so far has been on a Pixel 10 Pro on wifi. That is not the target device and never was. A shared family phone is also worth one pass — the app assumes one person per handset.
  • Turn off web debugging, and prove it
    webContentsDebuggingEnabled is true in two files. It stays on while you test, and comes off in the same pass as the deploy.
    Why "prove it": a memory note once recorded this as done when it wasn't. Verify by attaching a debugger to the release build and failing to connect.
  • Seize your own phoneUnusual
    Take a test handset with a real church on it, and examine it the way a competent examiner would. Write down everything learned.
    Why: we make claims about what a locked app reveals. This is the only way to know whether they are true. Whatever it finds becomes the honest wording — and one already-known answer is that the app's presence and church membership are visible to a proper inspection.
  • Deploy both halves together
    The relay update and a fresh signed app build. They must go out as a pair.
    Why paired: the console and app now share a secret older versions don't understand. Deploying one without the other blanks names or empties group chats.
    • Wipe test data after the merge, before anyone joins
    • Rebuild + release-sign the app
    • Update the relay box
    • Fresh install against the live system, as a stranger would
    • Confirm the internal-docs leak is closed on the deployed box
  • Get the release signing key off this machine
    If the keystore is lost, no existing install can ever be updated again. Not by us, not by anyone.
    Why now: this is the one failure with no recovery path at all, and it gets worse with every phone that installs the app. Two copies, two places, at least one offline.

Running the pilot Pilot

Two or three churches — enough to break assumptions that only hold for one congregation. Different sizes, different leadership, at least one where you are not in the room.

  • Write the honest limits into the joining packNo code
    Plain words: what this protects you from, and what it does not.
    Why it's a pilot task and not a nicety: a member who understands the limits makes better decisions than one trusting a claim we can't back — and someone in that room may end up explaining it to an officer. Must say plainly that a PIN does not defeat a court order.
  • Run a "bad week" drill with each churchUnusual
    Sit down and rehearse: the bank account is frozen; a steward's laptop is taken; the relay box is seized. What happens in the next hour, and who does what?
    Why: this is the product's entire purpose and it has never been exercised. A drill will find missing tools faster than any feature list — and it tells you which roadmap items below are real and which are theatre.
  • Try to enumerate the pilot churches from outsideUnusual
    Given only the public site and a relay address, how much can an outsider learn — which churches exist, how many members, who they are?
    Why: "unenumerable" is the claim the name encryption exists to support. Success means finding nothing. Do it before real congregations depend on it, and repeat it after every change to what the relay serves — that is exactly how the internal-docs leak was found.
  • Answer the data-protection question before a church asks itNo code
    A UK church using this is a data controller. Members have rights over their data — access, correction, deletion — and the church has retention duties, especially around children.
    Why it's awkward and worth solving: encryption helps enormously — the relay holds ciphertext — but "we can't read it" is not the same as "we have no obligations". A one-page answer a church trustee can hand to their safeguarding officer removes a real adoption blocker.
  • Get a UK lawyer's read on steward exposureNo code
    Specifically: what a steward can be compelled to produce, and what happens if they genuinely cannot.
    Why: we are asking volunteers to hold a key that opens their congregation. They deserve to know their position before they accept it, and the answer shapes the multisig design.
  • Decide how a church reaches you when it breaks
    One route, stated in the app, that works when the app is the thing that's broken.
    Why: with three churches this is a phone number. It is worth writing down anyway, because the answer at thirty churches is different and you'll want to know when it changed.
  • Capture what people actually do, without instrumenting themUnusual
    No analytics, no telemetry, no phone-home. Ask instead: a short call at week one and week four with each church.
    Why this constraint is a feature: a product whose whole pitch is "we can't see your data" cannot ship usage tracking. Talking to three churches is better research than a dashboard anyway — and it stays honest at thirty.
  • Write the pre-mortemUnusual
    One page: it is three months from now and the pilot failed. What happened?
    Why: the likely answers ("names showed as Anonymous and it looked broken", "the steward lost the key", "nobody could get a Lightning address") are all cheap to prevent now and expensive to fix in front of a congregation.

Making one target into many After pilot

The strategy is to make attacking churches one-by-one uneconomic. That is an infrastructure and governance problem more than a code problem.

  • Public relays in more than one jurisdiction
    Additional boxes like the current one, run in countries whose legal systems do not readily cooperate with each other.
    Why "jurisdiction" not "location": three servers in three data centres under one legal regime is one demand, not three. The property you want is that no single court order reaches them all. Pick on legal diversity first and latency second.
    • Choose jurisdictions on legal grounds, written down
    • Stand up the first additional public relay
    • Prove a church survives losing its primary relay — pull the plug and watch
    • Confirm operators are separate people, not all you
  • A warrant canary on every public relayUnusual
    A signed statement, refreshed on a schedule, saying no demand has been received. Its absence is the signal.
    Why it fits here: operators can be forbidden from disclosing a demand. They cannot generally be compelled to keep signing something untrue. Cheap to build, and it makes the multi-jurisdiction spread legible to churches rather than a claim they take on faith.
  • Prove the app installs with no app store and no internetUnusual
    From a memory card, a local box, or one phone to another in the room.
    Why: app stores are a single point of removal — the tidiest way to delete this product from a country is one takedown request. The QR identity transfer already proves the pattern works; this extends it to the app itself. It is also how it reaches somewhere with no data.
  • The paper fallback packUnusual
    What a church can print and keep in a drawer: join slips (built), key recovery sheets, and a one-page "the internet is off, what still works" card.
    Why: paper does not have a battery, cannot be remotely wiped, and is not seized as a data-bearing device in the same way. For a congregation, it is also the format their least technical member is least frightened of.
  • Answer the bus questionUnusual
    If this dev box dies, or you are unavailable for six months, what happens to the churches already running?
    Why it belongs on a roadmap: churches are being asked to move their whole community onto this. The honest answer today is that they keep running but stop receiving updates, and the signing key is the pinch point. Write that down and decide whether it is acceptable.

Being findable, without making a target list Ongoing

Marketing wants churches named and visible. Security wants them unenumerable. This tension is real and should be decided deliberately rather than drifted into.

  • Decide whether a church is ever named publiclyDecide first
    Case studies, logos on the site, testimonials, a launch post thanking the pilot churches.
    Why this comes before the accounts: every social channel below will want exactly that content, and a named pilot church is a published list of congregations using privacy tooling. Whatever you decide, decide it once, in writing, before the first post.
  • Claim the names everywhere, post on few
    Register the handles now so nobody else does; actively run only what you can sustain.
    Why: a dormant account someone else takes is worse than no account — for a product about trust, an impostor account is a genuine attack, not an annoyance.
    • Facebook + Instagram — where UK congregations and their leaders actually are
    • X + YouTube — reach, and the home for walkthrough videos
    • Nostr + Bluesky — aligned, and dogfooding: the app already speaks Nostr
    • LinkedIn — administrators, denominational staff, trustees: the people who sign off
    • Cross-link every account from the site so the real ones are provable
  • Run the news channel on your own infrastructure first
    Announcements go out over TrinityOne itself, and are mirrored to the social accounts — not the other way round.
    Why: if the announcement channel lives on a platform, the platform can end your relationship with your own users. Doing it this way also makes the product demonstrate its own argument every time you post, and gives you a real user's view of the app weekly.
  • The walkthrough videos
    Connecting a church to a relay, first launch, restoring an account. The written guides exist; the visual parity does not.
    Why: "connect a church to a relay" was the single biggest documentation gap, and it is the step where a leader most likely gives up alone at a laptop on a Saturday.
  • The FAQ page
    Long agreed, never built. The honest-limits answers from the joining pack belong here too.

The two big builds after the pilot After pilot

Both have written plans on branches. Both are answers to "what happens when they go around the encryption instead of through it".

  • Split the church key — reference/MULTISIG-CHURCH-KEY.md
    Encrypting names moved the roster's readability rather than removing it: from the relay box to one steward's laptop. Splitting the key means a demand needs several people in several places.
    The risk to respect: lose a quorum of pieces and that church's records are unopenable by anyone, for ever. That is the feature working. It is why nothing is destroyed until a real reassembly has been rehearsed — and why it must not be in the pilot.
    • Decide how pieces reach holders (paper / scan / locked to their account)
    • Decide whether a holder must be a TrinityOne user
    • Confirm there is no escape hatch — any recovery route can also be compelled
    • Build phases A–E
  • Lightning giving — reference/PAYMENTS-LIGHTNING.md
    Not how a UK church gets funded — congregations don't hold bitcoin. It is how a church still receives money the week its bank account is closed over its beliefs.
    The blocker: a church has nowhere to get a Lightning address, and nothing else can be tested until one does. The option that fits the thesis is the church's own relay box issuing it — no account for anyone to close. Same move as making that box the push carrier.
    • Answer the address question — this gates everything
    • Pilot churches complete + verify setup, without opening it to the congregation
    • Receipts and records: a gift the church can't see is worse than no gift
    • Live exchange rate — no currency symbol on screen until then
    • The second rail: publish ordinary bank details alongside
    • Gift Aid — the biggest financial lever for a UK church

Beyond one congregation After pilot

Two asks that turn out to be the same shape: a network over several churches (Regions Beyond over Trinity Littlehampton), and church-adjacent organisations — a foodbank, a youth charity, a mission agency, a student group, a church plant. Both are "TrinityOne for something that isn't a single congregation", and both are mostly a language and permissions problem rather than a new product.

  • Keep the direction of the relationship — a church declares its networkDon't break
    Today a church publishes "we belong to X". A network cannot claim a church.
    Why this matters more than it looks: it is the answer to "can a denomination watch its churches?" — no, unless invited, and the invitation is revocable by the church alone. Any oversight feature that quietly reverses this direction turns the product into the thing it was built against. Whatever gets built below, this stays.
  • Design what a network console is actually for
    The identity switcher and network view exist; there is no network dashboard behind them. Before building one, decide whether the model is oversight or support.
    The design question: Regions Beyond over Trinity Littlehampton is an apostolic sphere, not a head office. That argues for a console that resources — shares a safeguarding policy, a teaching series, a rota template, a trusted relay — rather than one that reports. A dashboard of attendance and giving across member churches is the obvious build and probably the wrong one; it also creates exactly the aggregated target this product avoids.
    • Ask two real network leaders what they actually need, before designing
    • Default: a network sees no individual member of any church, ever
    • Share downward: policies, series, templates, a relay churches can trust
    • If any aggregate is shown, the church opts in per figure and can see what it shares
    • Leaving a network is one tap and needs nobody's permission
  • Handle the ordinary messiness: a church in more than one network
    A congregation may sit under a denomination, a local churches-together group, and a mission partnership at once — and a leader may serve several churches.
    Why to check early: the member side had exactly this bug last week — a person in two churches went nameless in one because the relay kept a single slot. The same single-slot assumption is worth hunting for on the network side before anyone hits it.
  • Make the vocabulary switchable
    "Church", "congregation", "steward", "member", "giving" are written into every screen. A foodbank has volunteers and donors; a charity has trustees and supporters.
    Why it is worth doing properly: an organisation that reads "your church" on every screen concludes the product is not for them within a minute, whatever the feature list says. This is a labelling layer chosen at setup, not a fork — and it is the cheapest way to widen who this serves without diluting what it is.
  • Map the tools onto how a charity actually works
    Much of it already fits — Finance models restricted and designated funds today, which is the hard part and the thing generic tools get wrong.
    Where it fits and where it doesn't: teams map to projects, rotas to volunteer shifts, safeguarding is if anything more demanding. What is missing is trustee-shaped: who is a trustee, an annual return, a reserves policy, and Gift Aid — the same Gift Aid work the payments plan needs, which makes it one job serving two audiences.
    • Trustees as a distinct role, not just "stewards"
    • An export a trustee can take to an accountant or the regulator
    • Gift Aid — shared with the payments plan, build once
    • Check safeguarding still holds when there is no congregation, only volunteers
  • Notice that adjacent organisations are often the ones under most pressureUnusual
    A street-preaching ministry, a pregnancy counselling service, a group supporting converts from other faiths — these attract attention a Sunday congregation does not.
    Why this belongs on the roadmap and not just in marketing: if the hardest cases are adjacent organisations rather than churches, then serving them is not a widening of scope — it is the core use case arriving under a different name, and their requirements should shape the product rather than be retrofitted to it.

Deliberately not doing Standing

As useful as the task list. Each of these will be suggested by someone reasonable.

  • Usage analytics of any kind
    A product whose pitch is "we can't see your data" cannot ship telemetry. Talk to churches instead.
  • Holding anyone's money, even briefly
    No balance, no float, no custody, for any convenience gain.
  • A central directory of churches
    It is the target list. Discovery has to work without one.
  • Any recovery route we control
    If we can restore a church's data, we can be compelled to.
  • Naming a protection more strongly than it deserves
    The standing rule at the top of this page.
