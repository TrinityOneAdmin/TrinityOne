About

A private home
for the whole church.

TrinityOne is a private, self-run place for the whole life of a church — Scripture, fellowship and care — built on open standards the church itself holds.

It's free and open-source, and it serves one body in very different places: the church that must gather in secret under pressure, and the church that is free to gather in the open. No accounts, no company in the middle — just your people, private and secure.

Not a platform. A protocol.

Like email — an open standard anyone can run. No company in the middle, nothing central to seize, and the church holds its own keys.

Private by design

No email or phone number to sign up. Messages are encrypted, the member roster is sealed, and safeguarding for children is built in.

Free, and open

Open-source (AGPL). Self-host it on a small computer, or just use the app. Free is the model — not a trial that ends.

The two are joined

Not two products. One Church.

A church with steady internet, a little money and no fear of the door can run TrinityOne on its own small computer — a relay, the box that carries a church's messages. From that same box it can offer a home to churches that could never run one. It is a switch in the console: Offer to host other churches. Bandwidth you already pay for becomes somewhere else's Sunday.

It runs the other way too. The app is small, works offline and asks for no account because it had to serve a church on an old phone where being found is dangerous. Every church gets that engineering, comfortable ones included. The core is free and open under the AGPL and stays that way; paid add-ons for churches that can afford them are how we intend to keep it so.
