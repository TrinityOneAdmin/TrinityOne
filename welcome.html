Why we built this

There are rooms where the Church meets in whispers

In much of the world, gathering as believers is the most ordinary thing — a building, a sign at the road, a Sunday morning. But for a great many of our brothers and sisters, it is not ordinary at all.

For them, being online is a risk — every account, every message, every list of who-belongs can be watched, demanded, or used against them. The connection is thin and comes and goes.

We could not build for the comfortable Church and quietly leave these believers out. We built for them first.

Not because the comfortable Church doesn't matter — it does, dearly — but because a tool forged for the persecuted Church turns out to be good and safe for the Church anywhere. The same thing that shelters a congregation in hiding gives a free church a fuller week's life, and the same protection it is wise to keep even where you still gather openly.

Not a platform. A protocol.

A platform is something you join — a company owns the servers, holds your data, sets the rules, and can lock you out tomorrow. A protocol is something you run, like email. TrinityOne is a protocol: you hold your own keys, you can host it yourself, and there is no "in" for anyone to shut you out of.

Private by design

No email or phone number to sign up. Direct messages are encrypted, group rooms can be, the member roster is sealed, and safeguarding for children is enforced by the server — not just hidden in the app.

Not two products. One Church.

A church with steady internet and no fear of the door can run TrinityOne on its own small computer — and from that same box offer a home to churches that could never run one. Bandwidth you already pay for becomes somewhere else's Sunday.

Run the whole thing on a box you own

A church can hold its own messages, members and records on hardware in the building. Or use ours, and move later without anyone losing anything.

Why these translations? (And do you have the NIV?)

Short answer: the NIV, ESV and NLT are not open licences — they are owned by publishers who charge for the right to include them in an app, per user. We would have to pass that cost to your church, every year, for ever. TrinityOne is free and stays free, so we ship the translations that are free to give away: the WEB, the KJV, the ASV and over a thousand more, in many languages.

If your church reads from the NIV on a Sunday, nothing stops you — bring your own Bible, and use TrinityOne for everything around it. We would rather be honest about a real limitation than quietly charge you for it.

Can't reach this page? Install from any church

Every TrinityOne church running its own relay serves a complete copy — the app, the console, the Bibles. If this page doesn't load where you are, install from another church's relay: same app, same signing key, byte-for-byte identical. That is by design; it was built to travel with the people.

The one rule: install from a TrinityOne relay you asked for — this page, or a church's own address. Don't install a file someone sends you as an attachment or on a memory stick unless you can check where they got it. A changed copy looks exactly the same, and your phone will not warn you the first time you install it.

Moving across, without the upheaval

Start with one group, keep your current tools running alongside, and move at your own pace. Because it's self-custodial, your data is always yours to take.

  1. Start with one group. Your leadership team or a single life group, for a week or two. Most of TrinityOne turns on and off under Settings → Features, so you can begin with just chat and a Bible.
  2. Re-create your groups. In the console → Groups, make a room for each one: open for anything church-wide, invite-only for a closed group.
  3. Bring your people across. Drop your join link into each existing WhatsApp or Facebook group; for anyone not in those, print the invite poster — one QR the whole room can scan.
  4. Move your records. Giving history imports from the CSV your current software exports. Your directory fills itself in, because everyone arrived already named.
  5. Go live, gently. Keep the old tool read-only for a season if it helps people settle. There is no moment where everything has to switch at once.
